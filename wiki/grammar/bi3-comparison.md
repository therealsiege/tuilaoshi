# 比 Comparison

- **Pattern**: A + 比 + B + Adjective
- **Structure**: A 比 B Adj

## Explanation

比 (bǐ) is used to make direct comparisons between two things. The item that comes first (A) is the one being described as "more" of the adjective. Unlike English, there is no separate comparative form of the adjective — 高 (gāo) means both "tall" and "taller" depending on context. Do NOT use 很 in a 比 comparison, because 很 implies a neutral or absolute degree, which conflicts with the comparative meaning.

To express a specific degree of difference, add a quantity phrase after the adjective: A 比 B 高五厘米 ("A is 5 cm taller than B").

## Example Sentences

- 他比我高。(Tā bǐ wǒ gāo.) — He is taller than me.
- 今天比昨天冷。(Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng.) — Today is colder than yesterday.
- 坐火车比坐飞机便宜。(Zuò huǒchē bǐ zuò fēijī piányi.) — Taking the train is cheaper than flying.
- 中文比法语难。(Zhōngwén bǐ Fǎyǔ nán.) — Chinese is harder than French.

## Negation

Use A 没有 B + Adj to express "A is not as [adj] as B":
- 我没有他高。(Wǒ méiyǒu tā gāo.) — I'm not as tall as he is.
- 今天没有昨天冷。(Jīntiān méiyǒu zuótiān lěng.) — Today isn't as cold as yesterday.

Alternatively, use 不比 to mean "not more ... than" (implies roughly equal):
- 他不比我高。(Tā bù bǐ wǒ gāo.) — He's not taller than me (we're about the same).

## Common Errors

- Adding 很 in a 比 sentence: ✗ 他比我很高。 ✓ 他比我高。(Tā bǐ wǒ gāo.)
- Reversing the order: ✗ 我比他高 when you mean "he is taller than me." ✓ 他比我高。(The first item is the "more" one.)
- Using 不 instead of 没有 for "not as ... as": ✗ 我不比他高。(This means "not more than," not "less than.") ✓ 我没有他高。(Wǒ méiyǒu tā gāo.) — I'm not as tall as him.
