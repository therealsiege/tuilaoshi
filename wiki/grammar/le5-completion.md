# Verb + 了 (Completed Action Aspect)

- **Pattern**: Subject + Verb + 了 + Object
- **Structure**: S + V + 了 + O

## Explanation

了 (le) after a verb marks the perfective aspect — it signals that an action has been completed or realized. This is NOT the same as English past tense. 了 focuses on the completion of an action regardless of when it happened. A completed action can occur in the past, present, or future. The sentence 我吃了饭再走 means "I'll leave after I've eaten" — a future completed action.

There are actually two types of 了: verb 了 (directly after the verb, marking completion) and sentence-final 了 (at the end, indicating a change of state or new situation). This page covers verb 了.

## Example Sentences

- 我吃了午饭。(Wǒ chī le wǔfàn.) — I ate lunch.
- 他买了三本书。(Tā mǎi le sān běn shū.) — He bought three books.
- 她喝了一杯咖啡。(Tā hē le yì bēi kāfēi.) — She drank a cup of coffee.
- 我们看了那个电影。(Wǒmen kàn le nàge diànyǐng.) — We watched that movie.

## Negation

Negate with 没(有) (méi(yǒu)) before the verb. Drop 了 when negating:
- 我没吃午饭。(Wǒ méi chī wǔfàn.) — I didn't eat lunch.
- 他没有买书。(Tā méiyǒu mǎi shū.) — He didn't buy books.

**Note**: Never use 不 to negate a completed action. 不 is for habitual actions and future intentions.

## Common Errors

- Using 不 to negate completed actions: ✗ 我不吃了午饭。 ✓ 我没吃午饭。(Wǒ méi chī wǔfàn.)
- Keeping 了 with 没: ✗ 我没吃了午饭。 ✓ 我没吃午饭。
- Confusing 了 with past tense: ✗ 昨天我很忙了。 ✓ 昨天我很忙。(Zuótiān wǒ hěn máng.) — Adjective states don't take verb 了.
