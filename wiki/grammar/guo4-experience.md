# Verb + 过 (Experiential Aspect)

- **Pattern**: Subject + Verb + 过 + Object
- **Structure**: S + V + 过 + O

## Explanation

过 (guo) after a verb indicates that an action has been experienced at some unspecified point in the past. It corresponds to English "have (ever) done something." The focus is on whether the experience has occurred in one's life, not when or how many times. 过 is often used in questions with 没有 to ask "have you ever...?"

## Example Sentences

- 你去过中国吗？(Nǐ qù guo Zhōngguó ma?) — Have you ever been to China?
- 我吃过北京烤鸭。(Wǒ chī guo Běijīng kǎoyā.) — I've eaten Peking duck (before).
- 她学过法语。(Tā xué guo Fǎyǔ.) — She has studied French (at some point).
- 我们看过这个电影。(Wǒmen kàn guo zhège diànyǐng.) — We've seen this movie (before).

## Negation

Negate with 没(有) before the verb. Keep 过 in the negative form:
- 我没去过日本。(Wǒ méi qù guo Rìběn.) — I've never been to Japan.
- 他没有吃过寿司。(Tā méiyǒu chī guo shòusī.) — He has never eaten sushi.

## Common Errors

- Dropping 过 in negative: ✗ 我没去中国。(This means "I didn't go to China" — a specific event.) ✓ 我没去过中国。(Wǒ méi qù guo Zhōngguó.) — I've never been to China.
- Confusing 过 with 了: ✗ 你吃了北京烤鸭吗？(Asks about a specific instance.) ✓ 你吃过北京烤鸭吗？(Asks about lifetime experience.)
