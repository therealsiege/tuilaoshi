## Example Sentences

- 服务员，请给我们菜单，我们要点菜。(Fúwùyuán, qǐng gěi wǒmen càidān, wǒmen yào diǎn cài.) — Waiter, please give us the menu, we'd like to order.
- 这道菜又辣又好吃。(Zhè dào cài yòu là yòu hǎochī.) — This dish is both spicy and delicious.
- 吃完了，我们买单吧。(Chī wán le, wǒmen mǎidān ba.) — We've finished eating, let's get the bill.

## Usage Notes

In Chinese restaurants, it is common to call out 服务员 (fúwùyuán) to get a waiter's attention — this is not considered rude. 买单 (mǎidān) and 结账 (jiézhàng) both mean "pay the bill," with 买单 being more casual and common in southern China and 结账 more standard. The four basic taste words are 酸甜苦辣 (suān tián kǔ là — sour, sweet, bitter, spicy), a set phrase that also metaphorically describes life's ups and downs. When describing food, 好吃 is for food (literally "good to eat") and 好喝 (hǎohē) is for drinks ("good to drink"). The measure word for dishes on a menu is 道 (dào): 一道菜 (one dish) or 份 (fèn) for portions: 一份菜单 (one copy of the menu).
